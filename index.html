Positioning ThatHolds Up Indoors

Estimate a moving target’s X–Y position from a few fixed anchors, and stay accurate when signals are noisy, missing or blocked.

Get Started
The problem

Indoor location works well in demos and less well in real buildings.

Bluetooth and ultra-wideband can locate people, wheelchairs, infusion pumps and pallets inside a building. In practice, deployments rarely look like the lab: budgets limit how many anchors can be installed, corridors fill with people, and walls bend or block radio paths.


When a measurement passes through a wall, the distance it reports is longer than the real one. A model that trusts every reading will place the target in the wrong room. When an anchor drops out, many systems lose the track entirely.

Limited anchors

Fewer reference points means less redundancy. One bad reading has a larger effect on the position estimate.

Noisy signals

Range and RSSI values fluctuate with body position, furniture and interference, even when the target is still.

Non-line-of-sight bias

Obstructed paths add a consistent positive error to the measured range, which silently pulls the estimate off course.

Intermittent data loss

Packets drop and anchors briefly disappear. The system has to continue sensibly instead of resetting.

What we are solving

Lowest practical error on unseen data, with limited and unreliable anchors.

Our solution estimates X–Y position from range and/or signal-strength measurements, operates with a reduced number of reference points, reduces the influence of probable non-line-of-sight readings, uses motion history to improve stability, and reports every position together with an uncertainty value. Accuracy is compared across different anchor densities and noise levels, so a facility can see what it gains from each additional anchor before buying it.

How it works

From raw measurements to a confident position.

Ingest

Timestamped range/RSSI from whichever anchors are reachable, plus known anchor coordinates.

Qualify

Each reading is scored for likely obstruction or bias, using residuals against neighbouring anchors and recent motion.

Estimate

A learned model combines trusted readings with temporal filtering to produce an X–Y position.

Report

The position is returned with confidence, the anchors that influenced it, and a plain-language reason.

Capabilities

Seven additions built on top of the core challenge.

01

AI Anchor Placement OptimizerHigh impact

Recommends the best locations for fixed anchors to maximize coverage and reduce positioning error. Simulate different placements and compare the results before installation.

02

Obstacle-Aware Position Recovery

Detects when walls or obstructions make measurements unreliable, then uses alternative anchors and movement history to recover the location.

03

Crowd-Aware Tracking

Simulates crowded corridors where signal quality deteriorates, identifies crowded zones and shows where tracking accuracy may degrade.

04

Predictive Positioning

Estimates where a target is likely to move next, helping maintain a stable track during brief signal interruptions.

05

Self-Learning Error MapHigh impact

Records where the system repeatedly makes errors, learns location-specific corrections, then validates them on separate trajectories.

06

Emergency Tracking Mode

Simulates an emergency in a hospital or warehouse, prioritizes reliable tracking of selected assets and flags areas where estimates become uncertain.

07

Automatic Stress Testing

Generates thousands of simulated combinations of noise, missing anchors and obstructions to find the conditions under which the algorithm breaks.

Where it is useful

Places where a few metres matter.

Locate equipment and staff across long racked aisles where metal shelving distorts signals.

Support wayfinding and space planning in offices, campuses and public buildings.

Find wheelchairs, pumps and monitors quickly, and guide visitors who need assistance.

How we validate

Built to be judged on hidden data, not on a favourable test set.

  • Synthetic venues with known anchor coordinates, simulated trajectories, controlled noise, packet loss and non-line-of-sight bias.
  • Evaluation on different paths, noise levels and obstruction patterns from those used in training.
  • Comparison across infrastructure densities, with the baseline and our method reported side by side.
  • Stress-test reports that state the assumptions and limits of the method clearly.
